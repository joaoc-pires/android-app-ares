Restoring an old bicycle

By Rita Mendes

Last winter I found a steel touring frame from the early eighties in a neighbour’s garage. The paint was flaking, the chain had rusted solid and both tyres had long since given up, but the frame itself was straight and the lugs were beautiful.

The first job was stripping everything down. Every bolt went into a labelled tray, and I photographed each stage so that reassembly would not turn into a puzzle. Penetrating oil and patience freed the seat post after two evenings of gentle persuasion.

With the frame bare I could finally inspect it properly. A little surface rust inside the bottom bracket shell cleaned up with a wire brush, and a coat of frame saver should keep it from coming back. The headset cups were pitted, so they were replaced.

Rebuilding the wheels took the longest. I laced new rims onto the original hubs, which spun smoothly after fresh grease and new bearings. Truing them by hand on a homemade stand was slow but deeply satisfying.

The finished bike rides wonderfully: quiet, comfortable and surprisingly quick. It is a reminder that good things are often worth repairing rather than replacing.
